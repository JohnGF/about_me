title: "Bio-Hacking & BCI Gaming"
description: "Leading scalable R&D pipelines for engineering students at LASIGE Summer of Research."
tags: ["DIY Hardware", "Unity/Python", "R&D Mentorship"]
problem: "Traditional BCI systems have a steep learning curve and high barrier to entry for engineering students. Furthermore, AI calibration in BCIs is notoriously tedious and disconnected from engaging real-world tasks."
tasks:
  - "Establish a mentorship pipeline to guide students in building custom, non-invasive neurotech hardware."
  - "Design an optical glucose sensor to study metabolic effects on brainwaves ('Sugar Rush')."
  - "Develop a rhythm-driven BCI game that gamifies the AI calibration process."
eureka: "By integrating the BCI calibration phase directly into a rhythm-based game in Unity, we eliminated the 'calibration barrier'. Users were so focused on matching the music that they generated highly distinct, noise-free motor-imagery signals naturally, without feeling like they were undergoing a tedious clinical calibration."
results: "The mentorship program resulted in functional DIY hardware (including a novel optical glucose sensor) and an engaging BCI game interface. The game significantly reduced calibration time while providing high-quality data for AI decoding."
